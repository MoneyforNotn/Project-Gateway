## Evidence labels used here

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Credible core** | Supported by broader evidence, though not necessarily by a direct Gateway trial |
| **Mixed / bounded** | Evidence exists, but effects are inconsistent, small, context-dependent, or method-sensitive |
| **Experience supported** | The reported class of experience can occur; its extraordinary interpretation is not thereby established |
| **Contested** | Serious positive evidence and serious methodological objections coexist |
| **Unsupported mechanism** | The proposed mechanism is not established by the evidence reviewed |
| **Philosophical / metaphysical** | Coherent as interpretation or worldview, but not demonstrated by the cited empirical evidence |

## Where the 1983 memo is strongest

The memorandum is strongest when it:

- describes the training sequence and its intended experiences,
- notices connections with relaxation, meditation, hypnosis, and biofeedback,
- treats self-knowledge and attention as trainable,
- proposes that claims should eventually be tested.

## Where it becomes weakest

The argument becomes progressively less secure when it:

- converts analogies into literal physical mechanisms,
- shifts between different meanings of “energy” without specifying units or coupling,
- treats similar numerical frequencies as evidence of communication,
- extends optical/brain “hologram” analogies into a universal information store,
- uses quantum terms without an experimentally specified quantum system,
- treats an unusual experience as evidence of information transfer,
- moves from nonchance statistical results to operational reliability.

## The Planck-length example

The memorandum's printed page 12 describes “Planck's Distance” as though it were a velocity threshold below which an oscillation leaves time-space.

That is dimensionally inconsistent: **Planck length is a length, not a speed**. An ordinary oscillator can have zero instantaneous velocity at a turning point without leaving spacetime.

This matters because it is not merely a disagreement in interpretation; it is a consistency error inside the proposed physical mechanism.

## Remote viewing: the calibrated position

The evidence review does **not** reduce remote viewing to “obviously fake,” nor does it call it established.

The record contains:

- favorable statistical assessments,
- positive meta-analytic results,
- critical reviews emphasizing independence and replication,
- concerns about judges, repeated participants, targets, and procedural artifacts,
- poor operational usefulness in the intelligence context.

The appropriate label is therefore **contested**, with a much higher bar for “reliable intelligence tool” than for “interesting average statistical anomaly.”

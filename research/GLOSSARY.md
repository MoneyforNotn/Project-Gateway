## Gateway practices

The descriptions in this group explain the training vocabulary. A useful interpretation of a practice is not the same as a clinical trial of that named practice.

<a id="term-gateway-experience"></a>

### Gateway Experience

Monroe Institute training intended to alter or expand consciousness. The package combines relaxation, audio, suggestion, imagery, attention, and repeated practice.

Evidence for one ingredient does not validate the complete course or its extraordinary claims. Read the [claim ladder][claim-ladder] and [Gateway concepts in testable language][concepts]. Historical source: [P02][p02].

<a id="term-focus-10"></a>

### Focus 10

The program label "mind awake, body asleep." A cautious interpretation is deep relaxation with retained awareness; sleep-onset imagery may occur.

The label is not established as one universal EEG state. Feeling deeply relaxed does not establish a particular sleep stage. See [Gateway concepts][concepts] and the [sleep discussion][sleep].

<a id="term-focus-12"></a>

### Focus 12

The program label for "expanded awareness," described in the memorandum as extending beyond ordinary perception. It can describe a change in attention, imagery, or felt spaciousness.

Access to external information is a separate claim requiring separate evidence. See [Gateway concepts][concepts] and [external-information standards][external-info].

<a id="term-focus-15"></a>

### Focus 15

A program label associated with timelessness and, in the memorandum, access to the past or experience outside ordinary time.

A changed sense of time does not establish access to historical events. Any claimed information must be independently checked. See [Gateway concepts][concepts].

<a id="term-focus-21"></a>

### Focus 21

A program label associated in the memorandum with access beyond time-space and toward the future.

Literal future perception and travel to other dimensions are not established by the sources reviewed. These Focus labels describe the program's claims, not validated physiological stages. See [Gateway concepts][concepts].

<a id="term-energy-conversion-box"></a>

### Energy Conversion Box

A visualization for setting aside concerns during a session. It can be interpreted as an attention or worry-deferral ritual: name a concern, imagine placing it aside, and return to it afterward.

This interpretation does not assume literal storage or conversion of physical energy. See [imagery and problem solving][imagery].

<a id="term-resonant-tuning"></a>

### Resonant tuning

Humming or vocalization intended to build resonance in the program's account. In ordinary terms, it combines extended exhalation, vibration, sound, and an attentional ritual.

The memorandum's proposed coupling to planetary fields remains unsupported. See [Gateway concepts][concepts] and [resonance claims][resonance].

<a id="term-rebal"></a>

### Resonant Energy Balloon (REBAL)

An imagined energy field surrounding and protecting the participant. Protective imagery may increase felt safety or agency.

A literal defensive field has not been demonstrated. The psychological interpretation is a plausible adaptation, not a direct trial result for REBAL. See [imagery and problem solving][imagery].

<a id="term-patterning"></a>

### Patterning

Using intention to shape desired future outcomes. Goal clarification, mental rehearsal, salience, motivation, and subsequent behavior are testable interpretations.

Thought alone changing outside events without a causal route requires separate evidence. See [Gateway concepts][concepts] and [imagery and problem solving][imagery].

<a id="term-problem-solving"></a>

### Problem solving

The memorandum describes receiving answers during expanded awareness. The exercise can be treated as incubation: define a problem, disengage, record later associations, then test them.

A useful answer does not establish that it came from an external information store. See [imagery and problem solving][imagery].

<a id="term-living-body-map"></a>

### Living Body Map

Imagery used in the memorandum to visualize and "heal" body systems. A cautious interpretation is guided imagery and attention to bodily sensations.

The sources reviewed do not establish disease treatment through imagined energy. This practice is not a substitute for medical treatment. See [Gateway concepts][concepts] and [imagery and problem solving][imagery].

## Audio, mind, and sleep

An auditory response, a report of relaxation, a verified sleep stage, and accurate concealed-target perception are different observations. Each needs a suitable measure.

<a id="term-binaural-beat"></a>

### Binaural beat

An auditory beat percept that can occur when different tones reach each ear. The perceived beat relates to the difference between the tones' frequencies.

Neural and psychological consequences vary by protocol. Hearing the beat does not by itself establish global brain synchronization or a useful outcome. See [what a binaural beat establishes][binaural] ([N01][n01], [N04][n04]).

<a id="term-ffr"></a>

### Frequency-following response (FFR)

A family of auditory neural responses that follow features of a sound stimulus. The memorandum describes the brain as "mimicking" an externally presented frequency.

An auditory response is not equivalent to global hemispheric synchronization. The response, the reported experience, and the practical outcome must be measured separately. See [audio evidence][binaural] ([N01][n01], [N04][n04]).

<a id="term-hemi-sync"></a>

### Hemi-Sync

A product and technique name. The memorandum proposes that audio produces matching EEG amplitude and frequency in the left and right hemispheres.

Generic binaural-beat research does not establish that full claim. Small Hemi-Sync clinical trials address narrow outcomes; they do not test the full Gateway sequence, remote viewing, or time travel. See [evidence specific to Hemi-Sync][hemi-sync] ([N05][n05], [N06][n06]).

<a id="term-hemispheric-lateralization"></a>

### Hemispheric lateralization

Differences in how particular functions or networks involve the brain's two hemispheres. The memorandum uses a much stronger analytical-left / intuitive-right division.

Some functions are lateralized, but the reviewed evidence does not support classifying whole people as globally "left-brained" or "right-brained." See [what hemisphere synchronization should mean][hemispheres] ([N13][n13]).

<a id="term-hypnosis"></a>

### Hypnosis

A practice using attention and suggestion to influence experience and behavior. Responsiveness varies across people and settings.

The memorandum's explanation that left-brain screening switches off is a period model, not the review's current explanation. Evidence for hypnosis in selected clinical contexts does not validate Gateway's mechanism. Hypnosis and sleep are discussed separately; neither a hypnosis label nor a relaxation report identifies a sleep stage. See [hypnosis][hypnosis] ([N08][n08]).

<a id="term-meditation"></a>

### Meditation

Practices that train attention or awareness. The review discusses focused attention, open monitoring, mantra practice, and related methods as distinct practices with different instructions and outcomes.

Evidence for a structured meditation program cannot simply be transferred to an audio-only Gateway package. See [meditation][meditation] ([N09][n09], [N15][n15]).

<a id="term-biofeedback"></a>

### Biofeedback

Training that gives a person information about a measured bodily process so they can practice influencing it. The review discusses heart-rate-variability and temperature feedback.

Learning to influence a measured response does not imply voluntary control over every organ process or access to a universal energy field. See [biofeedback][biofeedback] ([N07][n07]).

<a id="term-interoception"></a>

### Interoception

Perception of internal bodily sensations. In the review's practical discussion, interoceptive attention means noticing what is felt in the body during a session.

Record the sensation separately from its explanation. A felt flow or vibration does not, by itself, identify a physical energy source. See [imagery and problem solving][imagery] and [the uses of "energy"][energy].

<a id="term-rem-sleep"></a>

### REM sleep

Rapid-eye-movement sleep, a distinct sleep stage associated with vivid dreaming and skeletal-muscle atonia, or greatly reduced muscle activity.

The memorandum calls REM the deepest ordinary sleep. Modern staging distinguishes REM from [N3](#term-n3-sleep), the stage conventionally called deep or slow-wave sleep. See [REM sleep is not the deepest sleep][sleep] ([N11][n11]).

<a id="term-n3-sleep"></a>

### N3 sleep

The modern term for deep or slow-wave non-REM sleep. It is not central to the memorandum's model.

N3, REM, and a program's Focus labels should not be treated as interchangeable categories. See [the sleep discussion][sleep].

<a id="term-lucid-dreaming"></a>

### Lucid dreaming

Dreaming with awareness that one is dreaming. In the reviewed laboratory work, researchers verified REM sleep and used prearranged signals to check occasional communication with dreamers.

That result does not establish telepathy or consciousness outside the body. A lucid dream and an [out-of-body experience](#term-obe) also make different claims about what was experienced. See [sleep and lucid-dream verification][sleep] ([N11][n11]).

<a id="term-obe"></a>

### Out-of-body experience (OBE)

A subjective experience involving changed self-location or body ownership, often felt as being outside the physical body. The memorandum interprets it as consciousness separating from the body.

The experience can be sincere and vivid. Literal perception from an external location requires additional concealed-target evidence. Brain and multisensory studies address features of self-location without settling every spontaneous report. See [out-of-body experiences and their explanations][obe] ([N10][n10], [N14][n14]).

<a id="term-remote-viewing"></a>

### Remote viewing

A claim to obtain information about a distant or concealed target without ordinary sensory access. It is an external-information claim that can be tested with blinding, prospective records, and independent judging.

The literature remains contested, and the examined intelligence program had poor operational reliability. Research on remote viewing does not automatically test Hemi-Sync or Gateway training. See [the remote-viewing evidence][remote] and [operational usefulness][utility] ([P05][p05], [P06][p06], [P19][p19], [P20][p20]).

## Physics and philosophy

The same word can name a measured quantity, an analogy, or a felt experience. The memorandum sometimes moves between those meanings without demonstrating the connection.

<a id="term-energy"></a>

### Energy

The memorandum uses this word for physical energy, vitality, information, intention, consciousness, and a metaphysical ground of reality.

These meanings must be distinguished. A physical claim needs a defined quantity, units, and a way to measure it. A report of felt "energy" can be recorded as an experience without assuming a physical mechanism. See [how "energy" changes meaning][energy] and the [physics audit][physics-audit].

<a id="term-entrainment"></a>

### Entrainment

One oscillator influencing another toward synchronization. In this project, a physical or neural entrainment claim needs an explicit measure of what is synchronized and under which conditions.

Matching frequency numbers alone does not demonstrate coupling. An auditory response also does not establish whole-brain synchronization. See [binaural-beat evidence][binaural] ([N01][n01], [N04][n04]).

<a id="term-schumann-resonances"></a>

### Schumann resonances

Electromagnetic modes of the Earth-ionosphere cavity, driven mainly by lightning. The memorandum incorporates these resonances into a proposed thought-communication mechanism.

Similar frequency values do not demonstrate a channel between a brain and this planetary field. Coupling, signal strength, encoding, and decoding would need to be shown. See [similar frequencies and communication channels][resonance] ([P08][p08]).

<a id="term-hologram"></a>

### Hologram / universal hologram

The memorandum combines optical holography and ideas about distributed representation into a model in which consciousness can "read" information throughout the universe.

Optical holography and distributed representation are real concepts. Their use as an analogy does not establish a universal information store accessible to the mind. Modern holographic physics is also a distinct theory, not evidence for that access. See [holograms and the universal-hologram claim][hologram] ([P09][p09], [P11][p11]).

<a id="term-planck-length"></a>

### Planck length

A length scale of approximately 1.6 × 10^-35 metres. The memorandum treats it as though it were a speed threshold for leaving spacetime.

A length and a velocity have different dimensions. The constant does not establish a consciousness threshold. See [the Planck-scale error][planck] ([P12][p12]).

<a id="term-absolute"></a>

### The Absolute

The memorandum's infinite, timeless conscious ground of reality. This is a metaphysical or philosophical construct.

The physics in the memorandum does not establish it as an empirical conclusion. Readers can examine its meaning while keeping philosophical interpretation separate from experimental findings. See [spiritual and philosophical implications][philosophy].

<a id="group-evidence"></a>

## Reading the evidence

These terms explain the methods used throughout the [evidence review][review] and [methodology][methodology]. They help identify what a result tests and what remains uncertain.

<a id="term-subjective-experience"></a>

### Subjective experience

What a person reports feeling or perceiving: relaxation, imagery, altered time sense, disembodiment, or another state. It is the first layer in the project's claim framework.

A vivid report supports a claim about experience. It does not, on its own, determine the mechanism or verify concealed external information. See [the four layers of claim][layers].

<a id="term-mechanism"></a>

### Mechanism

The proposed process causing an experience or outcome. A mechanism should specify variables, causal links, measurements, and competing explanations.

A benefit during audio use does not identify which ingredient caused it. See [proposed mechanisms][mechanisms] and [the claim ladder][claim-ladder].

<a id="term-operational-definition"></a>

### Operational definition

A statement of how a concept will be measured or tested. For a synchronization claim, this includes the recording locations, frequency band, time window, analysis, and comparison.

The label "deep" or "expanded" alone does not specify a measure. See [hemisphere synchronization and measurement][hemispheres] and the [physics audit][physics-audit].

<a id="term-control"></a>

### Control / active control

A control is a comparison condition used to separate possible explanations. An active control gives participants a comparable activity rather than no intervention.

For audio practice, quiet rest and ordinary relaxing audio answer different questions. Matching session length and other conditions helps assess what the target audio adds. See [personal audio comparison][audio-comparison].

<a id="term-randomization"></a>

### Randomization

Using chance to assign conditions, their order, or concealed targets according to a plan. It reduces the opportunity for selection to follow expectations or predictable patterns.

Randomization does not replace blinding or checks for information leakage. See [concealed-target test design][target-methods].

<a id="term-blinding"></a>

### Blinding

Keeping relevant information from people whose knowledge could affect a test. In a concealed-target exercise, the participant and session helper should not know the target; judges should not know which candidate is correct while judging.

State who was blind and to what. "Blind study" alone leaves that unclear. See [concealed-target test design][target-methods].

<a id="term-expectancy"></a>

### Expectancy

The influence of what someone anticipates will happen. Knowing which audio is playing can affect the experience or its rating.

A personal comparison may reveal a useful preference while leaving expectancy unresolved. See [personal audio comparison][audio-comparison].

<a id="term-information-leakage"></a>

### Information leakage

Ordinary clues reaching a participant or judge despite an intended concealment. Filenames, thumbnails, reflections, helper reactions, timing patterns, reused targets, and feedback can all carry clues.

A result is harder to interpret if these routes were not controlled. See [external-information test requirements][target-methods].

<a id="term-preregistration"></a>

### Preregistration

Recording the hypotheses and study plan before collecting or examining the relevant results. For a concealed-target exercise, the plan should fix the number of trials and the scoring rule before feedback.

Preregistration makes later changes visible; it does not by itself guarantee a sound experiment. See [later evidence and methodological caution][caution] ([P22][p22]) and [test requirements][target-methods].

<a id="term-exploratory-confirmatory"></a>

### Exploratory and confirmatory work

Exploratory work looks for patterns or develops a hypothesis. Confirmatory work tests a specified prediction with a plan set in advance.

An interesting first series can guide a fresh test. Reinterpreting the same series after seeing its results does not supply that fresh confirmation. See [concealed-target test design][target-methods].

<a id="term-effect-size"></a>

### Effect size

A numerical description of the magnitude of an effect or difference. The reviews cited here use different measures, so a number needs its measure, outcome, and study context.

The remote-viewing meta-analysis's pooled effect is not an accuracy percentage. A pooled audio effect across several outcomes also does not say that every outcome improves by that amount. See [audio outcomes][binaural] and [remote-viewing effect estimates][caution] ([N02][n02], [P20][p20]).

<a id="term-confidence-interval"></a>

### Confidence interval

An interval accompanying an estimate to express statistical uncertainty under the analysis's assumptions. Read it together with the effect measure and the study design.

It does not account for every possible source of error, such as information leakage or a biased set of published studies. See [remote-viewing estimates and their limits][caution] ([P20][p20]).

<a id="term-significance"></a>

### Statistical and practical significance

Statistical significance concerns a result's compatibility with a specified statistical model. Practical significance concerns whether the result is useful enough to matter in a real decision.

A reported nonchance average effect does not guarantee reliable performance on an individual trial. See [operational usefulness][utility] and [the methodology's utility standard][practical-utility].

<a id="term-replication"></a>

### Replication

Testing whether a finding recurs in a new study. Independence matters: new participants, targets, experimenters, judges, and laboratories can help distinguish a repeatable effect from a repeatable artifact.

A meta-analysis combines earlier results; it does not independently reproduce their procedures. See [the 1995 disagreement][air] and [later evidence][caution] ([P05][p05], [P06][p06], [P20][p20]).

<a id="term-publication-bias"></a>

### Publication bias

A distortion that can arise when the results affect which studies become available. Missing null or unfavorable results can change the apparent balance of evidence.

Failure to detect publication bias is not proof that it is absent. See [later favorable evidence and caution][caution] ([P20][p20]).

<a id="term-systematic-review"></a>

### Systematic review

A review that uses a stated method to find, select, and assess studies addressing a question. Its scope and inclusion criteria determine which evidence it covers.

This repository is a targeted evidence review and source archive. It cites systematic reviews but does not claim to be an exhaustive systematic review itself. See [methodology scope][methodology] and [source hierarchy][source-hierarchy].

<a id="term-meta-analysis"></a>

### Meta-analysis

A statistical synthesis of results from multiple studies. Its interpretation depends on which studies and outcomes were included, their methods, and the quality of the underlying evidence.

A pooled result does not establish a shared mechanism or remove the need for independent replication. See [binaural-beat reviews][binaural] and [remote-viewing synthesis][caution] ([N01][n01], [N02][n02], [P20][p20]).

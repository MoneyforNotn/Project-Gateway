## The document

**Title:** *Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process*<br>
**Author:** Wayne M. McDonnell, Lieutenant Colonel, Military Intelligence<br>
**Date:** 9 June 1983<br>
**Visible organization:** Department of the Army, U.S. Army Operational Group, U.S. Army Intelligence and Security Command, Fort George G. Meade, Maryland

The memorandum describes McDonnell's effort to assess the Gateway Experience's “mechanics and ultimate practicality.” He states that he personally took Gateway training and then sought models from physiology, consciousness studies, theoretical physics, and related literature.

## Army authorship vs. CIA custody

The project uses the formulation:

> **“A U.S. Army assessment later released through CIA records.”**

That is more accurate than “a CIA paper proving Gateway.”

The CIA Reading Room identifier reflects archival custody/release. The government-preserved catalog record identifies the Army/Defense Department as the government author.

**Declassification does not validate the document's scientific propositions.**

## Supplementary complete facsimile

[Open the supplementary PDF](../source/02_Supplement_Complete_Copy_With_Page_25.pdf)

A separately circulating 30-leaf facsimile contains the missing printed page 25. Reporting examined for the research project states that a complete copy was supplied through the Monroe Institute and hosted through DocumentCloud.

This copy is used **only with explicit provenance labeling**. It does not silently replace the government release.

The sources reviewed do **not** establish why page 25 disappeared from the government copy. The project therefore does not infer censorship or deliberate suppression from the omission alone.

## Editable transcript policy

[Read the editable transcript](../transcript/03_Gateway_Process_Editable_Transcript.md)

The transcript:

- restores printed pages 17 and 18 to logical reading order,
- includes the separately sourced printed page 25 with an editorial provenance note,
- normalizes scan line wrapping, spacing, and split words,
- retains period spelling, capitalization, terminology, and factual claims,
- does not silently modernize the original document's science,
- preserves exhibit artwork as images and supplies editable captions/labels,
- summarizes repetitive release-control headers/footers instead of repeating them on every page.

The historical transcript and the modern evidence review remain separate on purpose.

## Why separation matters

The 1983 memorandum includes claims that are scientifically outdated, speculative, disputed, or internally inconsistent. Those statements belong in the transcript because the transcript is a historical source edition.

Corrections and modern interpretation belong in the evidence review.

This keeps the archive usable for both historical reading and modern analysis without rewriting the past to match the present.

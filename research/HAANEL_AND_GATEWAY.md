# Haanel and Gateway · What does an image change?

When Haanel asks you to imagine a battleship, he eventually sends you below deck, back to the ore, and into the decisions that got the ship built. The image is a way to see more causes. Haanel also argues that one Universal Mind forms reality and works through each person, so sustained thought brings about corresponding conditions. McDonnell proposes a different mechanism for a similar claim. In Focus 12, he says, a thought cast as an accomplished event can interact with a universal hologram and help bring the event about within a chosen time window. What could we observe if either account were right? [Haanel, Part 1 ¶24–31](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-master-key-system/part-one) · [Parts 7–8](MASTER_KEY_COMPANION.md#parts-5-10) · [Gateway §30B](../transcript/03_Gateway_Process_Editable_Transcript.md#30-advanced-techniques)

## What each claim needs

| Claim | A useful check |
|---|---|
| **Experience.** The scene felt present or changed what seemed possible. | Record the experience when it happens. Save later interpretations separately. |
| **Action.** Imagining an end changed a plan or helped someone act. | Keep the first plan, the revised plan, and what happened. Compare with a suitable alternative if you want to estimate the effect of the imagery. |
| **Concealed information.** Impressions identify a target concealed by the protocol. | Fix the impression and scoring rule before feedback. Conceal the target from participants and relevant staff. A single match can occur by chance. Check whether accuracy exceeds chance across trials and recurs in an independent replication. |
| **Direct influence.** A directed intention changed an external outcome through a route other than ordinary action or information. | Specify the target, controls, timing, and a result that could count against the prediction. Compare prospectively and seek independent replication. |
| **Mind-first philosophy.** Mind is fundamental to what exists. | Examine the premises, what the account explains, and how it compares with other accounts. An experiment can address any specific prediction the account entails. |

In the course's fictional map case, the visitor sees a new arrow after a custodian shows them a side entrance. The lobby is locked, so the visitor needs help and the result falls short of the mapmaker's goal of unaided arrival. The mapmaker can trace how an imagined detail changed the edit. The visit leaves open whether the arrow changed what the visitor did or whether the outcome would have differed without the imagery. ## The individual and the whole

Haanel's spiritual *I* is the subject that notices thoughts and feelings, directs attention, and acts. He identifies this self with an expression of the Universal Mind. If people share one underlying Mind, another person's agency becomes part of the same whole. That helps make sense of why he values cooperation and service and objects to trying to dominate another person's will. What follows from sharing a ground with other people, and what further premise would give any one person the capacities of that whole? [Haanel, Part 4](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-master-key-system/part-four) · [Parts 12–13 in the companion](MASTER_KEY_COMPANION.md#parts-11-16)

McDonnell distinguishes the Absolute from a universe in motion and from the hologram through which events can, on his account, be represented or accessed. He also gives individuality a particular fate. In §§26–27, a consciousness returning to the Absolute retains its memories and the separate identity those memories confer, while losing the power to generate independent thought or exercise independent choice. Haanel emphasizes the relation of the individual to one creative Mind. McDonnell asks what perception and identity could be like after a merger. Read the two accounts for where they put memory, will, and the distinction between people. [Gateway §§23, 26–27](../transcript/03_Gateway_Process_Editable_Transcript.md#23-absolute-in-perspective) · [Philosophical background on idealism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/idealism/)

Haanel urges readers to keep unwelcome observations in Part 13. In Part 24 he locates spiritual Truth in developed consciousness and later calls on readers to weigh evidence. A spiritual conviction can have authority in someone's life while a claim that it cured an illness or changed a distant event makes a prediction for other people to check. The first can be discussed through experience, tradition, and argument. The second needs an outcome others can assess. William James's lectures on healthy-mindedness and the sick soul give a historical comparison for the role of conviction and the cost of leaving adverse experience out of the account. [Haanel, Part 13](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-master-key-system/part-thirteen) · [Part 24](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-master-key-system/part-twenty-four) · [William James, *The Varieties of Religious Experience*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/621/621-h/621-h.html)

## Attention and imagery

Haanel starts with stillness, then asks readers to inhibit thoughts, release muscular tension, and let go of preoccupations. Gateway describes setting aside concerns in an imagined container, resonant humming, an affirmation, sound, and progressive relaxation. The six-week course borrows the distinction between noticing and following a thought. In one laboratory sequence, Wegner and colleagues found that trying to suppress a specific thought increased its recurrence later. Hasenkamp and colleagues distinguished wandering, noticing, shifting, and sustaining attention in their study of experienced meditators. These are specific tasks with their own conditions. [Haanel, Parts 1–4](MASTER_KEY_COMPANION.md#parts-1-4) · [Gateway §§28–29](../transcript/03_Gateway_Process_Editable_Transcript.md#28-gateway-method) · [Wegner et al., 1987](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/DANWEGNER/pub/Wegner%2CSchneider%2CCarter%2C%26White%201987.pdf) · [Hasenkamp et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21782031/)

The companion follows Haanel from remembered details to imagined continuations, unfolding sequences, and deliberate transformations. The course allows words, diagrams, and spatial relationships. Keogh and Pearson found little imagery-based priming in their group with self-reported aphantasia, while spatial imagery ratings showed a different pattern. A useful map can begin as a picture, a set of relations, or a written route. Compare what the reader produces with the object or events it is meant to describe. [Haanel, Parts 5–10](MASTER_KEY_COMPANION.md#parts-5-10) · [Keogh and Pearson, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29175093/)

## Planning and action

In an exam-preparation study, Pham and Taylor found that imagining the process helped students study and improved their grades. Oettingen and Mayer found that positive expectations and positive fantasies had different associations with later effort and outcomes. Wang and colleagues found a small average benefit from combining a desired future, a real obstacle, and an if–then plan across 21 studies. The results varied, and possible publication bias qualified the estimate. These findings motivate a course exercise that records both the end and the next action. They address planning in particular settings. [Pham and Taylor, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167299025002010) ([PDF copy](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/59faaf5b01b9500001e95457/5bc55b26e337d65684a37d55_Pham%20%26%20Taylor%2C%201999.pdf)) · [Oettingen and Mayer, 2002](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/publications/motivating-function-thinking-about-future-expectations-versus-fantasies) · [Wang et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/)

The scale of the instruction matters. Lally and colleagues observed growing self-reported automaticity when people repeated a behavior in the same context, with substantial variation. Wood and colleagues found that repeating a broad positive self-statement could worsen immediate feelings for some participants with low self-esteem. Dixon and colleagues found associations between endorsement of manifestation, optimism about success, and certain risk-related judgments. Those associations leave the direction of causation open. A course prompt can ask for a modest, observable response and still give Haanel's account of aspiration and service a fair reading. [Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674) ([University of Surrey record](https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/How-are-habits-formed-Modelling-habit/99783513802346)) · [Wood et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19493324/) · [Dixon et al., 2025 issue](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/01461672231181162)

## Intuition and concealed information

Haanel treats intuition as independent of memory and experience and distinguishes inspiration from clairvoyance. McDonnell's problem-solving method expects an answer immediately or later, sometimes as a symbol. His explanation invokes access through expanded consciousness. Record the arrival of the idea, check whether it works, then ask where its information could have come from. [Haanel, Parts 17–20](MASTER_KEY_COMPANION.md#parts-17-20) · [Gateway §30A](../transcript/03_Gateway_Process_Editable_Transcript.md#30-advanced-techniques) · [Gateway §33](../transcript/03_Gateway_Process_Editable_Transcript.md#33-information-collection-potential)

Baird and colleagues reported a benefit from an undemanding break for previously seen creative problems. McDaniel and colleagues found no reliable difference between assigned break conditions in their preregistered writing study, although mind wandering correlated with one improvement measure for the same prompt. Kahneman and Klein describe how intuitive expertise can grow where cues are predictable and feedback gives people a chance to learn. These findings give us plausible routes to consider when an answer arrives suddenly. For concealed information, those same ordinary routes become alternatives to control. [Baird et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22941876/) · [McDaniel et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12241419/) · [Kahneman and Klein, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016755) ([PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/))

The Transparent Psi Project and subsequent replication studies did not reproduce Bem's original above-chance result for guessing a later randomized target. A subsequent study confirmed an exploratory *below*-chance result, which did not recur in a larger follow-up. The outcome applies to that future-guessing task. A remote-viewing protocol or a test of intention changing an event would require a different design. [Kekecs et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9890107/) · [Walleczek et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335330) · [Remote-viewing evidence review](04_Gateway_Process_Evidence_Review_and_Learning_Guide.md#remote-viewing-and-the-standard-of-external-verification)

## Direct influence and healing claims

If a specific directed intention changes an event independently of ordinary action, a study needs to say which event, how often, and under what conditions. A meta-analysis of random-number-generator studies reported a very small average effect with substantial variation and a pattern compatible with publication bias. A later online study with 12,571 participants favored its no-effect model for the effect it specified. A preregistered study using personality-related stimuli found that participants high on a measure of dependent traits encountered more trait-related sentences than expected in one of three experiments. A published critique disputes the analysis and inference, and the authors responded. These studies test particular random-number tasks. Haanel's universal law and McDonnell's holographic proposal would need independently reproducible predictions of their own. [Bösch et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16822162/) · [Maier et al., 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00379/full) · [Jakob et al., 2024](https://journals.lub.lu.se/jaex/article/view/23809) · [Critique](https://journals.lub.lu.se/jaex/article/view/25420) · [Response](https://journals.lub.lu.se/jaex/article/view/25782)

The texts also make historical claims about healing. Wager and colleagues linked experimentally induced placebo analgesia to changes in pain experience and associated brain activity. Benson and colleagues found that assigned intercessory prayer did not improve complication-free recovery among coronary bypass patients uncertain whether they would receive it. Relief, coping, tissue repair, and recovery from disease are different outcomes. The companion reads the healing passages as historical and theological material. [Haanel, Parts 22–24](MASTER_KEY_COMPANION.md#parts-21-24) · [Wager et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14976306/) · [Benson et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16569567/) · [NCCIH guidance](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety)

## Purpose

This project is a **targeted evidence review and source archive**, not an exhaustive systematic review or a claim to have reanalyzed every underlying dataset.

Its central methodological choice is to stop treating “Does Gateway work?” as a single question.

## Four layers of claim

Every major claim should be placed in one or more of these layers:

### 1. Subjective experience

**Question:** Did people report or exhibit a particular state or experience?

Examples:

- deep relaxation,
- vivid imagery,
- altered time sense,
- bodily vibration,
- felt expansion,
- lucid dreaming,
- disembodiment / changed self-location.

Useful evidence includes controlled phenomenology, behavioral reports, sleep staging, EEG/physiology, and replication across people.

### 2. Proposed mechanism

**Question:** What process caused the experience or outcome?

Examples:

- auditory frequency-following responses,
- expectancy,
- suggestion,
- attentional absorption,
- autonomic change,
- vestibular/body-representation changes,
- brain-network dynamics,
- the 1983 memo's proposed resonance and holographic mechanisms.

A mechanism requires more than an outcome. It should specify the variables, causal links, measurements, and competing explanations.

### 3. External information

**Question:** Did the state reveal information that could not have arrived through ordinary channels?

Examples:

- concealed-target identification,
- remote viewing,
- veridical OBE perception,
- prediction of a future event.

These claims require prospective recording, sensory isolation, blinding, decoys/controls, independent judging, complete reporting, and ideally independent replication.

### 4. Practical utility

**Question:** Is the effect reliable enough to matter in real decisions?

A small statistical anomaly is not automatically a useful method. Operational use requires accuracy, calibration, low false-positive rates, robustness across people/settings, and the ability to identify correct information **before** feedback.

## The claim ladder rule

Evidence does not automatically “flow upward.”

A study showing reduced pain during audio use does not establish remote viewing. A neural response to an auditory beat does not establish whole-brain synchrony. Whole-brain synchrony, even if demonstrated, would not by itself establish access to hidden information.

This prevents two opposite errors:

- **Halo reasoning:** one real scientific concept makes neighboring claims feel validated.
- **Wholesale dismissal:** one bad physics argument is used to deny that altered states or useful relaxation effects occur.

Each proposition gets its own test.

## Source hierarchy

The project generally gives greater weight to:

1. primary government records for provenance and historical claims,
2. original peer-reviewed research for experimental claims,
3. systematic reviews and meta-analyses for broader evidence patterns,
4. official scientific/health guidance for current safety context,
5. primary expert reports where a historical controversy itself is being documented,
6. secondary journalism for provenance or context when primary records are unavailable.

Popular books, interviews, and first-person accounts can be historically important without serving as validation of a biomedical or physical mechanism.

## Access-status discipline

The source inventory records whether a source was reviewed as:

- full text,
- abstract only,
- official summary,
- metadata/record,
- partial or failed access.

This distinction should survive future updates.

Do not write “the study showed…” with the same confidence when only an abstract was examined as when the full methods and results were reviewed.

## Evidence language

Preferred wording:

- “supports…”
- “is consistent with…”
- “reports…”
- “suggests…”
- “does not establish…”
- “remains contested…”
- “requires separate evidence…”
- “the sources reviewed do not establish…”

Avoid:

- “proves” unless the claim is truly deductive,
- “debunks” as a substitute for analysis,
- “CIA confirmed…”
- “science says…” without specifying which evidence,
- treating failure to detect publication bias as proof that publication bias is absent.

## How extraordinary-information claims should be tested

A useful concealed-target test should, at minimum:

1. predefine the target pool,
2. randomize target selection,
3. keep the participant and session helper blind,
4. record descriptions/drawings before feedback,
5. lock timestamps and retain misses,
6. use matched decoys,
7. use independent blind judging,
8. predefine the number of trials and scoring rule,
9. separate exploratory from confirmatory series,
10. replicate with new participants, targets, judges, and ideally laboratories.

Information leakage can occur through filenames, thumbnails, helper reactions, timing patterns, repeated targets, reflections, and feedback procedures.

## How physics claims are audited

Whenever a claim uses “energy,” “frequency,” “resonance,” “quantum,” or “holographic,” ask:

1. What physical quantity is meant?
2. What are its units?
3. How is it measured?
4. What couples system A to system B?
5. What signal strength is predicted?
6. What information is encoded?
7. What receives/decodes it?
8. What result would count against the proposed mechanism?

If those elements are absent, the language may still be useful as metaphor, phenomenology, or philosophy — but it is not yet a demonstrated physical mechanism.

## Update protocol

When adding a new paper or source:

1. Add it to `05_Research_Source_Inventory.json`.
2. Record full bibliographic metadata and URL/DOI.
3. Record access status.
4. State the study design.
5. State the finding without exaggeration.
6. State important limitations.
7. Identify exactly which Gateway claim(s) the source bears on.
8. Update the evidence review only if the new source materially changes the balance.
9. Preserve disagreement when reputable sources conflict.
10. Date the update.

The desired end state is not certainty. It is **calibrated uncertainty with traceable sources**.

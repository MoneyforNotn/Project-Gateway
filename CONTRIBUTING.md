# Contributing

Contributions are welcome from skeptics, practitioners, historians, neuroscientists, physicists, psychologists, statisticians, parapsychology researchers, archivists, and careful readers.

The repository is deliberately **not** organized around defending or attacking Gateway.

The governing question is:

> **What does the source actually support, and how strongly?**

## Good contributions

- transcript corrections tied to a visible scan page,
- stronger provenance for a historical claim,
- direct Gateway/Hemi-Sync experiments,
- preregistered replications,
- systematic reviews and meta-analyses,
- important null results,
- primary-source historical material,
- stronger methodological critiques,
- evidence that materially changes an assessment in either direction,
- corrections to our interpretation of physics/neuroscience/statistics.

## Before submitting a new scientific source

Please include:

- full citation,
- DOI/URL,
- source type,
- access status (full text / abstract / summary / metadata),
- study design,
- sample size where known,
- intervention and comparator,
- primary outcome,
- main finding,
- key limitations,
- exactly which Gateway claim it informs.

Whenever possible, add or update the corresponding entry in
`research/05_Research_Source_Inventory.json`.

## Claim-writing rules

Prefer:

- “supports”
- “reports”
- “is consistent with”
- “does not establish”
- “requires separate evidence”
- “remains contested”

Avoid:

- “CIA proved…”
- “science proves…”
- “quantum physics says…” without a specified result
- treating a subjective experience as an external-information result
- treating statistical significance as operational usefulness
- treating absence of evidence as evidence of a cover-up

## Transcript corrections

For transcription changes:

1. Name the printed page / source leaf.
2. Quote the current transcription.
3. State the proposed correction.
4. Attach or point to the visible source region.
5. Do not modernize period spelling or scientific terminology unless the change is clearly labeled editorial commentary.

## Evidence-review changes

A new source should change the prose only when it changes the evidentiary balance or materially improves accuracy.

Conflicting high-quality evidence should generally remain visible rather than being “resolved” by deleting one side.

## Extraordinary claims

For remote viewing, OBE target perception, precognition, or similar claims, give special attention to:

- preregistration,
- blinding,
- target randomization,
- information leakage,
- independent judging,
- complete trial reporting,
- exploratory vs. confirmatory analysis,
- independence of participants/targets/experimenters/judges,
- replication.

## Health claims

Do not present Gateway, meditation, hypnosis, imagery, or “energy” practices as substitutes for evidence-based medical treatment.

Claims about treating cancer or other disease require medical evidence independent of the 1983 memorandum.

## Copyright

Do not commit proprietary Monroe/Gateway audio or other copyrighted training material without permission.

Link to lawful sources rather than turning the repository into an audio mirror.

## Tone

Be precise without being contemptuous.

A person can have a profound experience while being mistaken about its mechanism. A critic can identify a real methodological problem without explaining every reported result.

That distinction is central to this project.
